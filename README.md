# PreserveDFQ
PreserveDFQ

## Abstract

Data-free quantization aims to obtain accurate low-bit models without access to the original training data. Typical two-stage DFQ methods first synthesize data using BN statistics of a pretrained model and then distill the quantized model with the synthetic data to mitigate quantization-induced performance degradation. However, existing synthesis methods do not fully exploit the pretrained model's perception of structural information, such as boundaries and textures, or explicitly model inter-class similarities, making it difficult to generate high-quality samples with informative structures and class-boundary information. During distillation, the distribution gap between synthetic and real data, together with the limited representational capacity of low-bit models, may cause activations to be progressively amplified and eventually clipped at the quantization boundaries, leading to unstable optimization. To address these issues, we propose PreserveDFQ. First, during data synthesis, we replace conventional Gaussian-noise initialization with random structural patterns to introduce structural priors such as boundaries and textures. Meanwhile, we characterize inter-class similarities based on the geometry of the classifier weights in the pretrained model and construct corresponding soft labels to improve the class-boundary quality of synthetic samples. Second, during distillation, we propose a Gradient Projection Straight-Through Estimator, which removes gradient components that drive activations further beyond the quantization boundaries, thereby suppressing progressive activation deterioration and improving the stability and accuracy of low-bit distillation. Furthermore, we theoretically demonstrate that GPSTE corrects these undesirable gradient components without compromising the descent property of the original distillation objective. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of PreserveDFQ. The proposed GSC-AL method is presented at https://github.com/Zdwsan/PreserveDFQ.
